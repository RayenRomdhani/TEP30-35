A country that runs on gas

Every evening, when the sun goes down and the air conditioners stay on, Tunisia's lights depend on gas plants, and most of that gas crosses the border from Algeria.

  • of the country's gas supply was burnt in power plants

If Tunisia uses one MWh less in 2030, what does its power system no longer have to buy, burn or build?

A planner's game, played 8,760 times

For each scenario, the model decides what to build and how to run every plant in every hour of 2030, at the lowest total cost. It must meet demand everywhere, keep renewables and a safety margin above the peak. Nobody tells it to build solar: it does because solar is the cheapest way through the year.

A 10% saving cancels a gas plant

To keep the safety margin above the 2030 peak, the plan needs MW of new gas turbines. Save 10% in every hour and the model builds none. That is why the first 10% is worth the most: EUR per MWh, then about EUR for each extra MWh.

Four years, built at Tunisia's pace

The 2030 above is one year on its own, with the model free to build whatever is cheapest. Here it plays 2027 to 2030 one year at a time. Each year starts with the plants of the year before, the committed projects switch on in the year they are due, and nothing is built faster than Tunisia has managed so far.

How far to trust it

First, make it reproduce 2024

Several lines match because the model was calibrated to match them. Gas burnt is the real consistency check; wind and solar were left as they came.

  • The 2030 answer has no speed limit

    On its own, 2030 adds about 6 GW of solar and wind in one step. The four-year pathway caps building at Tunisia's pace, and a saved MWh then avoids about twice the gas and CO2. Its caps are judgements, and it assumes committed projects arrive on time.

    Tested: savings worth more

  • Saving and shifting are free here

    The study prices the MWh, not the programme that saves it. Read the figures as the most a programme could cost and still pay off.

    Gross values

  • The model's peak comes two hours late

    Its summer peak lands around 19:00 in Tunis; the real 2024 peak came at 15:41. That moves when cooling savings and shifting land.

    Could push either way

  • Plants are more flexible than in reality

    No start-up costs, minimum output or breakdowns. Real plants would burn more gas around the evening ramp.

    Savings likely worth more

  • Imports are fixed

    They stay at their 2024 level every hour, so every MWh saved falls on Tunisian plants. The ELMED link to Italy is not modelled.

    A choice of scope

  • One weather year

    2013 weather, one-hour steps. A test at three-hour steps built 2.7% more solar and 3.9% more wind.

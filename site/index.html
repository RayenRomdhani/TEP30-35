01 The problem

A country that runs on gas

Of every 20 MWh generated in Tunisia in 2024, about 19 came from burning natural gas. Most of that gas comes from a neighbour, and demand keeps growing.

02 Key ideas

Five ideas carry the whole study

If you work in power systems, skim these. If not, they are all you need to follow the rest.

bolt

Energy is not power

Power (MW) is a rate; energy (MWh) is power × time. A country needs enough power for its worst hour and enough energy over the year: plants for the peak, fuel for the hours.

swap_vert

Efficiency vs flexibility

Efficiency removes MWh (same comfort, less electricity). Flexibility moves MWh in time, out of expensive hours into cheap, sunny ones.

shield

The reserve margin

Planners keep firm capacity above the peak. Here: above, from gas and hydro. Lower the peak and fewer plants are needed.

The merit order: who runs, and what it costs

running cost per MWh of electricity, gas at EUR/MWh

The last plant needed in an hour sets the price. Existing plants use the technology-data efficiency times a factor fitted to Tunisia's 2024 fuel use.

calculate

Avoided per MWh

The headline metric: (cost in the base case − cost with savings) ÷ MWh saved. The same for gas, CO2 and new capacity.

03 The model

A planner's game, played 8,760 times a year

For each scenario the model decides what to build (solar, wind, batteries, gas turbines) and how to run every plant in every hour of 2030, at the lowest total cost. Nobody tells it to build solar: it does because that is the cheapest way through the year.

06 Results

What the model found

Every number here is read from the study's result files.

1

A 10% saving cancels a gas plant

To keep the reserve margin, the base case builds MW of new gas turbines. Save 10% in every hour: the model builds none. That's why the first 10% is worth the most ( EUR/MWh, then ≈ for the next 5%).

2

A saved MWh cuts gas and renewables

Of the generation no longer needed in S1-15, would have been gas and renewables the model now doesn't build.

3

Timing changes what a MWh is worth

Take the same 15% from summer air conditioning first (only of it fits there) and each saved MWh avoids more gas and CO2.

4

Shifting demand three hours has real value

300 MW of shiftable load on top of S1-15 saves M EUR and Mt CO2 a year (gross: shifting is free in the model), about EUR per MW.

The gas price flips the story

With cheap gas, a saved MWh mostly displaces gas. With expensive gas, the system is already largely renewable, so it mostly displaces solar, wind and batteries.

07 How far to trust it

Calibrated, checked, and honest about its blind spots

Before 2030, the model had to reproduce 2024. Several lines below match because they were made to match: that's calibration, not proof.

What the model cannot see

speedNo speed limit on buildingtrending_up
The base case adds about 6 GW of solar and wind by 2030, far above Tunisia's recent pace. Read the renewable share as "what least cost would do", not a forecast.
paymentsSaving and shifting are freeinfo
The value per MWh is the most a programme could cost and still pay off. Shifting gets no capacity credit, which is conservative.
scheduleThe model's peak comes ~2 h lateswap_vert
Model summer peaks fall around 17:00 UTC; STEG's real peaks were at 14:41 UTC. This affects the cooling and shifting results.
settings_powerNo unit commitment or outagestrending_up
Plants run at any level, with no start-up costs or breakdowns. Real plants are less flexible, so more gas would run.
sync_altImports are fixedinfo
Imports stay at 2024 levels every hour, so every MWh saved falls on Tunisian plants. The ELMED link to Italy is not modelled.
wb_sunnyOne weather yearremove
2013 weather at 1-hour steps. A 3-hour test built 2.7% more solar and 3.9% more wind.
factorySteam units kept in 2030trending_up
STEG planned to retire 660 MW but has not committed. Retiring them would raise the value of saving.
cableGrid losses not modelledtrending_up
A MWh saved at the meter saves slightly more than one MWh at the plant.
